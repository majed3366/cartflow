# CartFlow Landing Page Constitution V1

**Status:** Foundational product constitution — governance only.  
**Surface:** Public marketing landing (`GET /` · `templates/cartflow_landing.html`)  
**Date (UTC):** 2026-07-29  
**Non-goals of this pack:** No redesign. No implementation. No copy rewrite until constitutional approval.

**Authority:** Normative law for the public landing page — mission, story, section ownership, truth, screenshots, copy, design, CTAs, and success.  
**Related:** Product promise (understand why customers buy, hesitate, or leave) · Dashboard / Knowledge Layer product surfaces (evidence for trust sections only).

This document defines **constitutional behaviour**. Landing work must conform after approval. Improvisation beyond this law is forbidden.

---

## 1. Mission

The landing page exists to achieve **one outcome**:

> Convince the right merchant that CartFlow understands their store better than any traditional recovery tool.

It is **not** designed to:

- Sell WhatsApp
- Sell widgets
- Impress with animations

It sells **confidence**.

---

## 2. Core Promise

Every visitor should leave with one clear belief:

> CartFlow doesn't just recover abandoned carts—it helps me understand why my customers buy, hesitate, or leave.

Everything on the page must reinforce this promise.

---

## 3. Positioning

### 3.1 Internal positioning (team / strategy)

CartFlow is positioned internally as:

> A Commerce Intelligence Platform with built-in revenue recovery.

- **Revenue recovery** attracts attention.
- **Commerce understanding** creates long-term value.

### 3.2 Landing disclosure law (binding)

**"Commerce Intelligence Platform" remains an internal positioning concept.**

The landing page must **not** introduce a new category label too early.

Instead:

1. Lead with the merchant's **problem**.
2. Let visitors **discover** the broader idea through the story.
3. Only by **Knowledge Layer** and **Dashboard** should they realise CartFlow is something broader than a recovery tool.

The narrative must feel **earned**, not claimed.

Forbidden early on the page:

- Category-first headlines (“Commerce Intelligence Platform…”)
- Claiming a new market category before the story has earned it

---

## 4. The Story

The page must tell a **story**.

Never a feature list.

The visitor should naturally progress through these stages:

### Stage 1 — "I have a problem."

- Lost revenue
- Abandoned carts
- Unknown customer behaviour

### Stage 2 — "My current tools don't explain anything."

- Discounts
- Reminders
- Guesswork
- No understanding

### Stage 3 — "CartFlow sees what I cannot."

- Recovery
- Behaviour
- Evidence
- Knowledge
- Recommendations

### Stage 4 — "I finally understand my store."

- Confidence
- Decisions
- Growth

---

## 5. One Purpose Per Section

Every section has **one job**.

Nothing more.

| Section | Responsibility |
|---------|----------------|
| Hero | Capture attention |
| Difference | Reframe thinking |
| Problem | Create urgency |
| Solution | Explain approach |
| Dashboard | Build trust |
| Knowledge Layer | Demonstrate intelligence |
| Merchant Journey | Show continuous value |
| Benefits | Reinforce outcome |
| FAQ | Remove objections |
| Final CTA | Convert |

If a section tries to do two jobs, it should be redesigned.

---

## 6. Truth Policy

- No fake numbers
- No fake merchants
- No fake charts
- No imaginary growth percentages
- No invented reviews
- No fabricated logos

Everything must be **real** or **clearly illustrative**.

Trust is more valuable than hype.

---

## 7. Visual Evidence Law

Every visual element on the landing page must strengthen **product credibility**.

Decorative visuals are permitted **only** when they support understanding of the product.

### Primary evidence (must dominate)

- Dashboard
- Merchant workflow
- Widget
- WhatsApp journey
- Knowledge cards

### Secondary (never compete)

Illustrations, 3D assets, and visual effects are **secondary**.

They must **never**:

- Compete with product evidence
- Obscure product evidence

If a decorative visual draws attention away from primary product evidence, it fails this law and must be removed or demoted.

---

## 8. Screenshot Policy

**Real screenshots only.**

Never decorate screenshots until they become impossible to recognise.

Screenshots should communicate:

- Understanding
- Evidence
- Operational clarity
- Decision making

—not merely UI beauty.

Screenshot Policy is subordinate to Visual Evidence Law (§7).

---

## 9. Copywriting Rules

The copy should feel like a **conversation**.

### Avoid

- Marketing clichés
- Buzzwords
- Corporate language
- AI jargon
- Exaggeration

### Prefer

- Clear
- Confident
- Specific
- Human

| Instead of | Say |
|------------|-----|
| "AI-powered revolutionary platform" | "Understand why customers hesitate before they leave." |

---

## 10. Design Philosophy

- Minimal
- Calm
- Confident

Whitespace is part of the design.

Do not fill space just because it exists.

Every element must earn its place.

---

## 11. Visual Hierarchy

The visitor should instantly understand, in order:

1. What CartFlow is
2. Why it matters
3. How it works
4. Why they should trust it
5. How to begin

No visual competition.

Only **one dominant message** at a time.

Visual Hierarchy governs **message** priority. Visual Evidence Law (§7) governs **credibility** priority of imagery. Both apply.

---

## 12. CTA Philosophy

Buttons should never feel desperate.

### Avoid

- Buy now
- Limited offer
- Don't miss out

### Prefer

- Start Free
- Book a Demo
- Explore CartFlow
- Understand Your Store

---

## 13. Emotional Journey

The visitor should experience:

Curiosity → Recognition → Confidence → Trust → Action

Not pressure.

---

## 14. Mobile First

The mobile version is the **primary** experience.

Desktop expands the experience.

It does not redefine it.

Every section must remain understandable without side-by-side layouts.

---

## 15. What We Never Compete On

We do **not** compete on:

- More features
- More dashboards
- More integrations
- More AI

We compete on **one thing only**:

> Helping merchants understand what is actually happening inside their store.

---

## 16. Success Metric

The page is successful if, after scrolling, a merchant naturally says:

> "This is different. It doesn't just help me recover sales—it helps me understand my business."

---

## 17. Approval Gate

Constitutional approval required before any landing redesign, section reorder, hero rewrite, or screenshot campaign that claims product capability.
